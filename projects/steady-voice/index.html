You are not alone, and it's not your fault

Stuttering is a neurological, often genetic condition affecting about 1% of adults — ~80 million people worldwide. It is not caused by anxiety, low intelligence, or anything you did. This toolkit helps you build skills and self-acceptance. There's no instant cure, but consistent practice and reduced avoidance genuinely help.

Echo — Altered Auditory Feedback

Hear your own voice slightly delayed and changed. For many people this naturally slows speech and eases blocks (helps roughly 1 in 3).

Use wired headphones. Without them you'll get loud feedback (echo squeal), and Bluetooth adds unreliable lag. Start at a comfortable volume.
Stopped

Tip: read a passage in the Reading tab while Echo is on. Effects can be temporary — treat it as practice support, not a cure.

Pacing — rhythm & syllable timing

Speaking in time to a beat is one of the strongest, best-evidenced ways to ease stuttering. Say one syllable per beat.

60 beats/min

Try: read slowly, one syllable per click. Start slow (50–70), then speed up as it feels natural.

How to use it

  • Pick a phrase and tap out a syllable on each beat: "I — would — like — a — cof — fee."
  • Keep it smooth and even — minimal stress differences between syllables.
  • Gradually raise the tempo toward natural conversational speed.

Breathing

Speech rides on breath. Belly breathing steadies airflow and lowers tension before you speak.

Ready

Follow the circle. Breathe from the belly, not the chest.

Paced reading

Confidence toolkit

Stuttering's hardest part is often fear and avoidance. These tools (from CBT & ACT) build courage and openness.

Avoidance-reduction ladder

List speaking situations you avoid, rate the fear (0–100), and check them off as you face them. Facing fears shrinks them.

    Self-disclosure builder

    Telling people you stutter ("advertising") reduces pressure and improves how listeners respond.

    Thought reframe (CBT)

    Swap an unhelpful thought for a balanced one.

    Learn — facts vs myths

    Accurate understanding reduces shame. Share these with friends, family, and colleagues.

    When to see a professional

    A speech-language pathologist (SLP) who specializes in fluency can tailor therapy to you — something no app can do. Consider one if stuttering affects your daily life, work, or wellbeing.
